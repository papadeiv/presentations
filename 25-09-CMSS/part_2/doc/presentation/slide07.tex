        % Title
        \frametitle{Critical slowing down}

        % Body
        \footnotesize

        Let's look at the linearised saddle-node system close to $\boldsymbol{x}_{\mu}^{(1)}$ but now let's inject additive Brownian noise with standard deviation $\sigma>0$

        \begin{equation*}
           \begin{cases}
                   dx_{t} = -2\sqrt{-\mu}x_{t}dt + \sigma dW_{t}\,, \\
                   dy_{t} = -\alpha y_{t}dt + \sigma dW_{t} \,.
           \end{cases}
        \end{equation*}

        \uncover<2->{
                Let's pick $\alpha=2\sqrt{-\mu}$ so that the decay rate is uniform in both $x_{t}$ and $y_{t}$.
        }

        \uncover<4->{
                Consider a circular region $\mathcal{B}_{\rho}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\mu}^{(1)})\subset \mathbb{R}^{2}$. How much time until my particles \textit{hit} $\mathcal{B}$?
        }
